# The Weight of the Miss

### Short Film Script — 8 shots — ~2 minutes total

**Logline:** A 25-year-old footballer sits across from a psychologist, wrestling with the penalty he failed to score in the World Cup final — until a chance encounter with a young fan outside the clinic forces him to confront what the game still means to him.

---

## 1. INT. PSYCHOLOGIST'S OFFICE — DAY — 0:00–0:15

A calm, modern office. Soft afternoon light filters through blinds. A worn leather chair faces a desk. LUCAS SANTOS (25, Brazil national team kit under an open jacket) walks in. He looks hollowed out — dark circles, unshaven. He sits heavily. Across from him, DR. AMARA OSEI (50s, composed, warm eyes) gestures to the chair. On her desk: a water glass, a notepad. She doesn't speak yet. Lucas stares at the floor.

> **DR. AMARA OSEI**
> Whenever you're ready.

---

## 2. INT. PSYCHOLOGIST'S OFFICE — DAY — 0:15–0:30

Close on Lucas. His jaw tightens. He opens his mouth. Nothing comes out. He looks at his hands — the hands that struck the ball wide of the post. He finally speaks, voice flat, rehearsed.

> **LUCAS**
> I've played since I was seven. Every day. My whole life has been one dream — to lift the trophy. And I had it. The final. The last penalty. Eighty thousand people screaming my n—

He stops. His voice fractures. He looks away. Dr. Amara waits. The sentence hangs, unfinished.

---

## 3. FLASHBACK. EXT. WORLD CUP FINAL STADIUM — NIGHT — 0:30–0:45

A wall of sound. Floodlights blaze. The camera finds Lucas at the penalty spot, 12 yards from goal. The stadium holds its breath. He steps back. Three steps. He places the ball. He looks up. The goalkeeper bounces on the line, shouting. Lucas breathes. He runs forward — and the shot cuts to black at the moment of contact. No sound. No result. Just silence.

---

## 4. INT. PSYCHOLOGIST'S OFFICE — DAY — 0:45–1:00

Back in the office. Lucas is leaning forward now, elbows on his knees. His eyes are wet but he won't let it fall.

> **LUCAS**
> I watched it from the hospital bed. Replay after replay. The goalkeeper diving the right way, and the ball — my ball — sliding past the post. Centimeters. My whole life, and it came down to centimeters.

He shakes his head slowly.

> **DR. AMARA OSEI**
> What did you feel in that moment?

> **LUCAS**
> Nothing. That's the problem. I felt nothing. Like I'd left my body.

---

## 5. FLASHBACK. EXT. BACKYARD DIRT PITCH — DAY — 1:00–1:15

A worn, sunlit backyard in a favela. Young Lucas (7), barefoot, in an oversized t-shirt, juggles a scuffed football with pure joy. His FATHER stands by the door, arms crossed, smiling. The boy dribbles past imaginary defenders and strikes the ball into a makeshift goal made of two bricks. He turns, beaming, arms raised. His father claps once, slowly, proud. The boy's laugh echoes — then cuts back to the present.

> **LUCAS (V.O.)**
> I used to tell my dad I'd win the World Cup for him. I was seven. I didn't even know what that really meant.

---

## 6. INT. PSYCHOLOGIST'S OFFICE — DAY — 1:15–1:30

Lucas wipes his eyes with the back of his hand. He looks at Dr. Amara, searching for something — permission to break, maybe. She doesn't rescue him. She waits.

> **LUCAS**
> Everyone says it's just a game. But it's not. It was everything I was. Without football... I don't know who I am.

A long pause. He doesn't finish the thought. He doesn't have to.

---

## 7. FLASHBACK. EXT. WORLD CUP FINAL STADIUM — NIGHT — 1:30–1:45

The same penalty, but this time we see the full arc. Lucas runs up. Strikes. The ball flies low, left — and whistles past the far post. The stadium collapses into groans and roars. Lucas drops to his knees. Teammates rush toward the goalkeeper in celebration — on the other side. He is alone at the center of the frame, on his knees, while the world erupts around him. A single tear runs down his cheek. He doesn't wipe it.

---

## 8. EXT. CLINIC ENTRANCE — LATE AFTERNOON — 1:45–2:00

Lucas exits the building into golden late-afternoon light. He pauses on the steps, breathing. On the sidewalk, a LITTLE BOY (7) in a Brazil jersey — two sizes too big, sleeves rolled up — tugs his MOTHER's hand and points. The boy's eyes go wide.

> **LITTLE BOY**
> _(whispering, then louder)_
> It's Lucas Santos! From the final!

Lucas turns. The boy breaks free from his mother and runs up, holding a cheap plastic football. He stops, suddenly shy. Looks at the ground, then back up. His eyes are bright with something Lucas has forgotten — wonder.

> **LITTLE BOY**
> I have your poster. In my room. You're my favorite player.

Lucas stares at the boy — same age he was in the backyard, same fire in the eyes. Something shifts behind his expression. Not a smile, not yet. But the weight moves, just slightly. He crouches down to the boy's level.

> **LUCAS**
> _(quietly)_
> Yeah?

The boy nods, clutching the ball. Lucas looks at it — scuffed, well-loved — the same way his was once. He reaches out, adjusts the boy's grip on the ball, shows him how to hold it. Their hands touch. The camera lingers on this: the penalty taker's hands guiding the child's.

> **LUCAS**
> Keep playing. Don't stop.

He stands. The boy beams. Lucas turns and walks away down the street, not looking back. The boy watches him go, then turns the ball over in his hands and kicks it into the air. Lucas hears the sound — the thud of leather — and for the first time, his pace slows. Almost imperceptibly. He keeps walking. The camera holds on the empty street as the sound of the ball echoes.
